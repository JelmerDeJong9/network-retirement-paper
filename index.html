Network Effects of Increases in the Statutory Pension Age

Jelmer de Jong · Daniël van Vuuren · Marike Knoef
Tilburg University, Department of Economics
Preliminary draft — October 2026.
This paper is work in progress. An updated working-paper version will be posted here when available.

Abstract

We study how the effects of increases in the retirement age are amplified through social networks in the Netherlands. Between 2012 and 2025, the Dutch government gradually increased the statutory pension age (SPA) from 65 to 67. We implement an instrumental variable strategy and exploit these gradual increases in the SPA to identify the causal effect of social networks on individual retirement behavior. Using novel Dutch network data covering the entire Dutch population we use three types of networks, workplace, and family. We find significant evidence of social network effects increasing the pension age. A 10 percentage point increase in the share of retired peers causes an individual to reduce working hours by around 0.5 hours per month and increases the probability of retirement by around 0.3 percentage points. We find that in the workplace peer effects are driven by same- gender peers. Back of the envelope calculations show peer effects amplify the direct effect of an increase in the pension age with a 3% delay in retirement and 6% additional working hours.
